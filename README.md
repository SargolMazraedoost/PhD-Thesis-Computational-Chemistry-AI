## Overview

This doctoral dissertation integrates data-driven machine learning and deep learning with first-principles computational methods to address two important challenges: chromatographic retention time prediction and protein binding affinity enhancement.

The research combines quantum chemical descriptors, molecular representations, predictive modeling, molecular dynamics simulations, and free energy calculations.

## Research Objectives

- Develop accurate cross-column quantitative structure–retention relationship models.
- Predict chromatographic retention times directly from molecular SMILES representations.
- Investigate the effects of protein mutations on binding affinity and complex stability.
- Integrate data-driven and physics-based approaches for molecular prediction.

## Research Components

### 1. Cross-Column QSRR Modeling Using DFT and Machine Learning

This study combines density functional theory-derived molecular descriptors and experimental chromatographic parameters to predict retention times across reversed-phase HPLC columns.

**Methods:**
- Density Functional Theory (DFT)
- Quantitative Structure–Retention Relationship (QSRR)
- Partial Least Squares (PLS)
- Ridge Regression
- Random Forest
- Gradient Boosting

**Reported results:**
- Predictive Q²: 0.989
- RMSEP: 0.749 min

### 2. SMILES-Based Retention Time Prediction Using Deep Learning

This study investigates chromatographic retention time prediction directly from molecular SMILES sequences, integrating a pretrained RoBERTa-based molecular representation with bidirectional LSTM layers.

**Methods:**
- Simplified Molecular Input Line Entry System (SMILES)
- Transformer-based molecular representations
- Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM)
- Deep Learning (DL)
- Transfer Learning (TL)

**Reported thesis results:**
- Dataset: METLIN SMRT
- Molecular records: 80,038
- Mean Absolute Error (MAE): 26.23 seconds
- Coefficient of Determination (R²): 0.91

### 3. Binding Affinity Enhancement of the MBD2–p66α Complex

This study explores protein–protein interaction stabilization through computational mutation screening, alchemical free energy methods, and molecular dynamics simulations.

**Methods:**
- Protein–Protein Interaction (PPI) Analysis
- In Silico Mutagenesis
- BeAtMuSiC
- Alchemical Free Energy Calculations
- Double-System/Single-Box (DSSB)
- Crooks–Gaussian Intersection (CGI)
- Molecular Dynamics (MD)
- Molecular Mechanics/Poisson–Boltzmann Surface Area (MM/PBSA)

**Key predicted binding-enhancing mutations:**
- K149I
- K149L
- K163L

These findings are computational predictions and do not establish experimental binding enhancement.
